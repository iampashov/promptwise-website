Project Introduction

What is PromptWise?

PromptWise: Writing for the Planet in the Digital Era is an Erasmus+ Mobility of Youth Workers project that brings together 36 youth workers from Germany, Greece, Portugal, Czechia, Italy, North Macedonia, Romania, Spain and Türkiye to explore AI literacy, responsible digital practices and environmental sustainability.

The project addresses a growing gap in youth work: while AI tools are becoming mainstream, most practitioners lack the skills to use them critically, responsibly and sustainably.

Participants are empowered through non-formal education methods such as hands-on workshops, prompt-writing labs, reading circles, field interviews, creative campaigns and a study visit. They gain practical skills in AI literacy, prompt engineering, sustainable digital habits, algorithmic bias detection and project design.

Coordinated by VMDO – Verbund sozial-kultureller Migrantenvereine Dortmund e.V. (Germany) and funded by the Erasmus+ Programme of the European Union.

Why We're Here

Project Objectives

Four competence areas every participant works on during the training and carries back into daily youth work.

AI Literacy

Improve participants’ ability to use Artificial Intelligence tools critically, safely and responsibly within their youth work practice. Learn what AI can and cannot do, how to identify bias, and how to apply AI ethically.

More info →

Prompt-Writing Competence

Develop practical prompt-engineering skills that participants can apply immediately: designing effective prompts for session planning, creating learning materials, and adapting AI outputs to specific youth groups.

More info →

Sustainable Digital Habits

Connect AI use to its environmental impact. Understand how AI systems consume energy, water and raw materials, and commit to reducing digital waste through concrete, measurable behaviour changes.

More info →

Inclusive & Learner-Centred Methods

Strengthen participants’ capacity to integrate digital tools in ways that are inclusive, accessible and learner-centred, so that technology serves people from all backgrounds, including those with fewer opportunities.

Objective

AI Literacy

We open the black box: data, training, pattern-matching and why a model can sound confident and still be wrong. Participants test real tools against real youth work tasks, compare outputs, trace where hallucinations and bias creep in, and agree on simple rules for safe, transparent use with young people. The outcome is a personal checklist for deciding when AI helps and when it should stay out of the room.

Add a photo
Objective

Prompt-Writing Competence

Prompting is treated as a craft. In the Anatomy of a Good Prompt workshop and the Prompt Studios, participants work with context, task, format, tone and ethical framing, rewrite weak prompts into strong ones and build a shared bank of prompts for session planning, learning materials, campaigns and reports. Every prompt is tested with a group and refined before it enters the PromptWise Toolkit.

Add a photo
Objective

Sustainable Digital Habits

Every generation costs energy, water and hardware. Participants measure their own digital footprint, read the UNEP and Sustainable AI research in reading circles, audit the venue in an eco-walk and translate the findings into ten concrete habits: fewer unnecessary generations, purposeful outputs, lighter files and greener cloud choices. The commitments are written down and followed up after the course.

Add a photo
Objective

Inclusive & Learner-Centred Methods

Digital tools must serve everyone. Through simulation games such as The Algorithm Decides and the jigsaw on AI literacy, participants see how biased or inaccessible tools exclude young people with fewer opportunities and learn learner-centred ways to design sessions where the technology adapts to the group, not the other way round.

Add a photo
Project activity

PromptWise Toolkit

The Toolkit is the main tangible result of PromptWise. Each international group co-writes one section: training modules on ethical AI use and prompt design, prompt templates for youth work, reflection and evaluation templates, eco-digital guidelines and case studies from the course. It is finalised in the Day 7 design sprint and published as an Open Educational Resource under a CC BY-NC-SA licence so any organisation can reuse and adapt it.

Add a photo
Project activity

Prompt Writing

From the first snowball discussion on how youth workers already use AI to the live demonstration of how context and tone change an answer, participants practise writing, testing and refining prompts in small groups. Before-and-after versions, failed chat transcripts and dot-voted favourites all feed the Toolkit’s prompt bank.

Add a photo
Project activity

Bias & Digital Citizenship

Participants examine examples of biased or misleading AI outputs, play The Algorithm Decides to feel the real-life consequences of unfair automated decisions, and connect the discussion to European values, digital rights and responsible citizenship. The aim is critical confidence: knowing when to question a tool and how to protect the young people you work with.

Add a photo
Project activity

Digital Sustainability

Reading circles on the UNEP report, the Sustainable AI paper, GreenComp and the European Green Deal, a digital footprint test, an eco-audit walk around the venue and a workshop on reducing data waste give participants the facts behind the slogan. The result is a set of measurable eco-digital practices for their organisations.

Add a photo
Project activity

“One Prompt, One Planet”

On Day 6 the group designs reels, posters and visuals for the One Prompt, One Planet campaign, tests them with local people in Istanbul and interviews passers-by about their AI habits. The materials and the social media posts live on in this website’s campaign section and keep spreading after the course under #OnePromptOnePlanet.

Project activity

Erasmus+ & Youthpass

A world café on the Erasmus+ programme and its priorities, daily reflection with the Learning Wall, 3-2-1 and other tools, and the ETS competence model for youth workers help participants name what they learn. On the last day every participant receives a Youthpass certificate mapping the competences gained.

Arrival

Arrival in Istanbul

Participants travel to Istanbul during the day. The host team welcomes everyone at the hotel, hands out the programme and practical information and the group shares a first dinner together.

Day 1

Welcome & Getting to Know

Name games, team building and a Mentimeter survey of fears, expectations and contributions open the course. The group agrees ground rules, discovers the project and the Erasmus+ programme in a world café and sets out on a city hunt with creative tasks. The evening is a welcome night in informal circles.

Add a photo
Day 2

Exploring AI

After the illustrated talk “How AI works in simple terms”, Step Forward If… makes personal AI habits visible and Human vs. Machine shows the hidden cost of generated content. In the afternoon national teams compare green and digital policies and complete a digital footprint test, ending with a vote on the best reduction measures. Intercultural night 1.

Add a photo
Day 3

Anatomy of a Good Prompt

A snowball discussion on AI in youth work leads into the Anatomy of a Good Prompt workshop with live demonstrations and before-and-after rewrites. Reading circles on sustainable AI research turn into short theatre plays, and an eco-audit walk documents sustainable and unsustainable behaviour at the venue. Intercultural night 2.

Add a photo
Day 4

Eco-Digital Practices

Short inputs on reducing data waste, greener cloud services and paper-free work are followed by Prompt Studios, where groups write prompts for sustainability campaigns and vote with dot stickers. The afternoon is a study visit to Deneyap Atölyeleri, public AI and robotics ateliers for young people. Mid-term evaluation with the pizza method and a Mentimeter quiz.

Add a photo
Day 5

AI Literacy & Storytelling

A jigsaw activity introduces AI literacy competences and examples of biased outputs prepare the simulation game The Algorithm Decides. In the afternoon participants brainstorm the digital competences youth workers need today, learn prompt engineering, context engineering and digital storytelling, and design a short social media campaign. Dinner outdoors in a natural park.

Add a photo
Day 6

One Prompt, One Planet

Groups produce videos, reels and posters for the One Prompt, One Planet campaign, test them with people in the city and interview locals about their AI use. An invited expert on green AI and sustainable digital habits speaks in the afternoon. Movie night on climate change and resource waste.

Add a photo
Day 7

Project Factory & Design Sprint

The Project Factory generates new Erasmus+ project ideas and concrete follow-up and dissemination plans for each organisation. The afternoon design sprint finalises the PromptWise Toolkit sections and the Workshop Replication Plan so that the results are ready for the local multiplier workshops.

Add a photo
Day 8

Evaluation & Youthpass

Guided reflection on what the group achieved, the ETS competence checklist, the Candy Game and art-based evaluation, the online evaluation form and the Youthpass ceremony. The course closes with a goodbye evening.

Departure

Journey Home

Participants leave Istanbul with their Youthpass, the Toolkit drafts and a commitment to run a local multiplier workshop within three months and to keep the campaign alive on social media.
